 ## Case Study: The Activity Selection Problem The Activity Selection Problem is a quintessential challenge in algorithm design. Imagine you have a set of n activities, each with a defined start time and finish time. Your goal is to schedule the maximum number of non-overlapping activities on a single resource, such as a conference room or a CPU core. Because you want to pack as many tasks as possible into a fixed schedule, you must make a sequence of local decisions. The core of the greedy strategy here is the greedy choice property: at every step, you select the activity that finishes the earliest. By choosing the task that ends first, you leave the maximum amount of time remaining for all subsequent activities. This is the optimal local choice, and mathematically, it is proven to lead to a globally optimal solution. > **[IMAGE GENERATION FAILED]** The Greedy approach selects tasks based on the earliest finish time to maximize capacity.
